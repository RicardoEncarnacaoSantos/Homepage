Cyborg (so much free time in the 90s...)
HELLO,

Time will tell if Artificial Intelligence is humanity's greatest achievement or merely overhyped ambition. Yet, regardless of its trajectory, AI will always retain the charming quality of existing between reality and dream, science and fiction. By definition, it mirrors something inherently alive: intelligence. Trying to create AI means attempting to replicate a phenomenon previously exclusive to biological brains crafted by millions of years of evolution. In that sense, AI may be a form of art after all—one that promises to surpass human intelligence while remaining intimately present in our daily lives (yes, Gemini helped me writing this :-)).

Back in the '90s, I programmed a checkers algorithm in Prolog—yes, I'm taking you back a bit. Every time I lost to it, I felt pure joy. Decades later, when Go master Lee Sedol lost to AlphaGo in 2016, his reaction was very different—he was devastated. But his objective was to win; mine was to build something capable of winning. Regardless of the game or the level of expertise, losing to our own creation isn't really a defeat at all. Unless, of course, we're talking about Skynet.

Also in the '90s, I coded my first ELIZA-inspired chatbot and practically forced my friends to talk to it. Despite being a basic—and admittedly primitive—piece of software with zero actual intelligence, it yielded surprisingly fun, surreal, and memorable conversations. That reveals another fascinating phenomenon beneath the surface of AI: our innate human tendency to project consciousness where none exists, attributing qualities like reasoning, empathy, or sarcasm to simple code. This is the ELIZA effect, and it remains equal parts amusing and unsettling. While rule-based engines like ChatScript took pattern matching to impressive heights, tech giants have since pushed large language models to the point of effectively conquering the Turing test. All of this in a mere three decades! Watching how our relationship with these artificial conversationalists evolves will be nothing short of mesmerizing.

A few years after the last AI winter thawed, I found myself working professionally with neural networks, deep reinforcement learning, and chatbots. It was thrilling to watch the world finally bet on AI, with major breakthroughs arriving year after year. It was during that golden window between 2015 and 2020 that I created this website to share my enthusiasm for where the field was heading. Since then, AI hasn't stopped evolving; the rapid rise of LLM architectures has rekindled both our greatest hopes and deepest fears about a powerful machine intelligence on the horizon.

What do I do? I'm a Senior Software Engineer who is constantly exploring Machine Learning. Over the years, I've worked in the AI space researching deep reinforcement learning for contact centers and developing chatbots using tools like Microsoft LUIS. At the same time, I’ve maintained a multi-channel Windows desktop application used by agents to handle voice, email, and instant messaging. Designing software that adapts to every customer's workflow is a massive challenge, but I’ve loved tackling it—building everything from custom layout and visual script editors to gamified leaderboards and 'smart inbound' intent identification systems.

I started working with cloud computing in 2018, using my background in machine learning to build a scalable, fault-tolerant AI service. The core was an autonomous system that periodically analyzed agent transcripts to power a real-time recommendation engine. During live chat sessions, it provided agents with instant suggestions based on conversational context and real-time input - handling five different languages seamlessly using built-in language detection.

The entire service ran in a Kubernetes cluster, using Azure Machine Learning (AML) to train, package, and deploy inference models as scalable pods. It relied on a blend of neural networks and traditional ML algorithms. Building an autonomous AI system like this was a fantastic challenge—especially automating fine-tuning without a 'human in the loop.' To solve it, the system spun up parallel training runs across reserved virtual machines to test hyperparameter variations, evaluated the results, and automatically deployed the winning model. Thanks to Azure Hyperdrive, we kept ML costs surprisingly low — and it was a blast to create.

Outside of AI and ML architecture, I've worked extensively across the modern cloud and infrastructure stack. My toolkit includes event streaming with Apache Kafka and Kafka Connect, full-stack observability using Prometheus and Grafana, and centralized logging via Graylog and Fluentd. I’ve also managed distributed storage in Azure (Blobs and Fileshares), worked with managed relational and NoSQL databases (PostgreSQL, MongoDB, Azure SQL), and embraced GitOps workflows using Flux. Tackling the challenges of high-throughput cloud infrastructure was just as rewarding as building the AI engines themselves.

I have also developed custom routing algorithms, developing the engine for an automated inbound routing solution built in partnership with Alcatel-Lucent Enterprise. The system was designed to be powerful, flexible, and intuitive—enabling users to graphically configure complex routing logic and monitor operations in real time.

I have also developed low-code tools for automated agents, including IVR and routing systems. The platform allowed users to build conversational voicebots and chatbots using speech recognition and text-to-speech features within an intuitive interface. Intent and entity management were handled seamlessly behind the scenes using Microsoft LUIS.

I am currently working with a multinational team to build a cross-platform UCaaS backend service. The platform provides a unified API that bridges diverse telephony systems across Linux, Windows, Kubernetes, and on-premises environments.

I am passionate about building software end-to-end, bringing ideas from initial research, brainstorming, and architecture through to final implementation and testing. Whether fine-tuning neural networks or refining high-adaptability user interfaces, I bring a methodical, empathetic, and creative approach to product design — always seeking opportunities to weave machine learning into modern software solutions.

I have a Licentiate degree in Information Systems and Computer Engineering from Instituto Superior Técnico (University of Lisbon).
